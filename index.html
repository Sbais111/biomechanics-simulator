Newton's 1st Law

Definition: An object remains at rest or in constant motion unless acted on by an unbalanced force.
How to use this simulator:
1. Observe the force arrows
2. Change push and resistance
3. Press apply force
4. Use motion outcome to answer questions
Compare the push arrow and the resistance arrow. Then decide what happens to the ball.

🧠 Apply your understanding

EXPLAIN / PREDICT
Balanced forces mean no change in motion.
Balanced forces mean zero net force, so motion does not change.
EXPLAIN / PREDICT
An unbalanced forward force makes the ball move.
Push greater than resistance creates an unbalanced force, so the ball starts moving or accelerates.
EXPLAIN / PREDICT
More resistance reduces net forward force.
Greater resistance reduces net force, so movement becomes slower or may stop.

Newton's 2nd Law

Definition: Acceleration depends on force and mass (a = F/m).
How to use this simulator:
1. Compare two objects
2. Change mass and force
3. Run simulation
4. Observe which accelerates more
Look at which object responds more when the force arrows are the same.

🧠 Apply your understanding

EXPLAIN / PREDICT
Same force on smaller mass gives more acceleration.
For the same force, the smaller mass accelerates more.
EXPLAIN / PREDICT
In a = F/m, bigger mass means smaller acceleration.
If mass increases and force stays constant, acceleration decreases.
EXPLAIN / PREDICT
Think about the object with less inertia.
A lighter object accelerates faster for the same force because it has less mass.

Newton's 3rd Law

Definition: Every action force has an equal and opposite reaction force.
How to use this simulator:
1. Watch action and reaction arrows
2. Change force
3. Run animation
4. Observe equal force interaction
Focus on the action arrow and reaction arrow during the collision.

🧠 Apply your understanding

EXPLAIN / PREDICT
The reaction arrow points back to the ball.
The backboard exerts a reaction force back on the ball.
EXPLAIN / PREDICT
Newton’s 3rd law gives equal magnitude force pairs.
Action and reaction forces are equal in magnitude and opposite in direction.
EXPLAIN / PREDICT
One acts on the board, the other on the ball.
They act on different objects, so they do not cancel on one object.

EXPLAIN / PREDICT
Use p = mv.
If velocity increases and mass stays constant, momentum increases.
EXPLAIN / PREDICT
Bigger mass means bigger momentum if velocity stays unchanged.
If mass increases and velocity stays constant, momentum increases.
EXPLAIN / PREDICT
Compare mass × velocity for both balls.
The larger momentum is whichever object has the larger mass × velocity product.

If contact time increases and force stays the same, impulse increases.

Follow-through mainly changes contact time.
Follow-through increases contact time, which increases impulse and change in momentum.

Conservation of Momentum

Definition: Total momentum before a collision equals total momentum after (if no external forces).
How to use this simulator:
1. Set masses and velocities
2. Run collision
3. Compare before and after momentum
4. Observe conservation
Watch both players before and after impact, then compare total momentum and final motion.

🧠 Apply your understanding

EXPLAIN / PREDICT
Think conservation law.
If no external force acts, total momentum is conserved.
EXPLAIN / PREDICT
Watch what happens after they stick together.
In this model, the players move together with one shared velocity after collision.
EXPLAIN / PREDICT
Equal and opposite momentum gives total momentum of zero.
Equal and opposite momenta give total momentum zero, so the shared velocity is zero.

Angular Momentum

Definition: Angular momentum = moment of inertia × angular velocity (L = Iω).
How to use this simulator:
1. Change body shape (inertia)
2. Toggle lock
3. Spin gymnast
4. Observe change in rotation speed
Angular momentum is the product of moment of inertia and angular velocity. When angular momentum stays constant, reducing moment of inertia increases angular velocity.

🧠 Apply your understanding

EXPLAIN / PREDICT
Bringing mass closer to the axis reduces moment of inertia.
A tighter tuck decreases moment of inertia because body mass moves closer to the rotation axis.
EXPLAIN / PREDICT
Use L = Iω.
If angular momentum is conserved, angular velocity increases when moment of inertia decreases.
EXPLAIN / PREDICT
Opening out increases moment of inertia.
Opening out increases moment of inertia, which reduces angular velocity and helps control the landing.

A lower coefficient of restitution lowers rebound height.
EXPLAIN / PREDICT
A value close to 1 means very elastic.
A coefficient close to 1 represents a highly elastic bounce with a high rebound.

Coefficient of friction

Definition: Ratio describing how much friction exists between surfaces.
How to use this simulator:
1. Change coefficient and mass
2. Apply force
3. Observe slipping or gripping
4. Compare forces
Maximum friction force = coefficient of friction × normal force. Sliding happens when required horizontal force is greater than maximum friction force.

🧠 Apply your understanding

EXPLAIN / PREDICT
Use μN.
Increasing the coefficient of friction increases the maximum friction force.
EXPLAIN / PREDICT
Compare the red and green arrows.
The block slips when required force exceeds maximum friction force.
EXPLAIN / PREDICT
Less grip means a smaller μ value.
A wet or smoother surface usually lowers the coefficient of friction.
